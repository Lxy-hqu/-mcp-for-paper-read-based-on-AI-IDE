# Multiple-paper synthesis

Give every paper its own evidence/claim trail. Versions of the same work are not independent replication; unread cited works are leads.

Compare question, assumption, mechanism, population/data/split, metric, compute, outcome and limitations. Do not rank incompatible metrics or average incomparable settings.

Investigate apparent disagreements through population, protocol, tuning, uncertainty and scope. Keep genuine disagreement visible instead of forcing one story. Explain compatible mechanisms, conflicting prerequisites, redundancy and interactions.

Proposals cite the enabling papers. Where feasible, compare baseline, each borrowed component alone and their combination under matched conditions. End with corpus-supported conclusions, unresolved questions, discriminating evidence and prioritized experiments.

“Absent from this corpus” is not “novel worldwide”. Current novelty claims require a separate primary-source search with an explicit coverage boundary.
