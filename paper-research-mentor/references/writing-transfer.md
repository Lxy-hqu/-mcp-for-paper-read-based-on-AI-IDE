# Source-bounded writing transfer

A single coauthored paper is a sample, not a stable personal-style label. Separate observed patterns from suggestions.

Use `writing` records with `category` (`term`, `collocation`, `sentence`, `paragraph`), `function`, abstract `template`, `conditions`, `strength`, `adaptation` and evidence IDs. Add translation, near-concept distinctions and misuse examples where useful.

Cover functions present in the sample: problem, gap, contribution, design rationale, data flow, comparison, ablation attribution, limitation and conclusion. No arbitrary quota.

- Terms: domain meaning, collocations, scope, non-equivalent near concepts and established names.
- Sentences: semantic slots, evidence licensing the verb, qualifications and an original adaptation example.
- Paragraphs: argument roles/transitions such as problem→consequence→requirement→design or claim→test→observation→bounded inference.
- Adaptation: identify slots requiring the user's own method and measured results. Use `[measured outcome]`, not invented achievements.

Use minimal necessary quotations with provenance and applicable limits; abstract/paraphrase the rest. Label short exact source examples, generated templates and user drafts separately. Never transfer another paper's findings into the user's voice.

Label strength as descriptive, comparative, associational, causal, theoretical or speculative. Association cannot license causal verbs. For multiple papers retain individual provenance and compare recurrence and exceptions, not just frequency.
